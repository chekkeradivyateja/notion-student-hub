## The Trap of Over-Optimization
One of the most common reasons ADHD focus systems fail is the 'Shiny Object Syndrome' of productivity tools. You spend three days configuring your Notion database, creating 15 custom properties, and automating workflows. By the time you’re ready to use it, you’re exhausted. The system itself has become a task.

**The Fix:** Start with a 'Minimum Viable System' (MVS). Use paper, a simple note app, or a basic list. Only add complexity when you have a stable routine. If your system takes more than five minutes to update, it is too complex for a high-energy day.

## Ignoring the 'Activation Energy' Gap
Many systems assume you will start a task once it is on the list. For ADHD brains, the hardest part is often the transition from 'thinking about doing' to 'actually doing.' A static list doesn't bridge this gap.

**The Fix:** Build 'entry points' into your system. Instead of a task that says 'Write Report,' break it down to 'Open Document and Type Title.' Use visual cues or alarms that signal the start of a work block. The system must reduce the activation energy required to begin.

## One-Size-Fits-All Scheduling
Standard planners assume you have consistent energy levels throughout the day. ADHD energy is often cyclical and unpredictable. Trying to force deep work into a low-energy slot leads to burnout and resentment toward the system.

**The Fix:** Adopt 'Energy Mapping' instead of time blocking. Track your energy for a week. Identify your 'Peak' and 'Slump' windows. Schedule high-focus work only in Peak windows. Use Slump windows for administrative tasks or breaks. Flexibility is not a failure; it’s a feature.

## Lack of External Accountability
ADHD brains often struggle with internal monitoring. If no one is watching, deadlines feel abstract. A focus system that relies solely on self-discipline is a ticking time bomb.

**The Fix:** Integrate external accountability into the system. This could be a 'body double' session (working alongside someone virtually), a public commitment, or a simple check-in with an accountability partner. The system should include a slot for social connection, not just task management.

## Forgetting the 'Shutdown' Ritual
Many systems focus heavily on starting and executing, but ignore the end of the workday. Without a clear shutdown, your brain stays in 'work mode,' leading to rumination and difficulty relaxing.

**The Fix:** Create a 10-minute shutdown ritual. Write down where you left off, list the first task for tomorrow, and physically close your laptop or move your workspace. This signals to your brain that work is done, allowing you to rest effectively.
