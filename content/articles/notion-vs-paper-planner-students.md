This debate usually turns into "digital good, paper bad" or the reverse. The
honest answer is that they fail in different ways, and the right pick depends
on how your brain and your semester actually work.

## Where paper wins

- **Zero distraction.** A notebook can't show you a notification.
- **Frictionless capture.** Writing "essay due Friday" takes two seconds and
  no app-switching.
- **Memory.** Handwriting genuinely helps some people remember deadlines and
  notes better than typing.

Paper's weakness is that it's a dead end: you can't filter it, search it, or
see "everything due this week across five classes" without flipping pages.

## Where Notion wins

- **One source of truth.** Assignments, notes, schedule, and grades live
  together and cross-reference each other.
- **Filtered views.** "What's due in the next 7 days, not done, sorted by
  date" is one click — impossible on paper.
- **It scales.** A heavy semester that would fill three notebooks is the same
  smooth database.

Notion's weakness is friction and distraction: if opening your planner means
opening a browser tab next to social media, the tool can become the
procrastination.

## A simple way to choose

- **Light course load, easily distracted by screens?** Paper, or paper for
  daily capture + a simple digital calendar for deadlines.
- **Heavy load, multiple classes, group projects, tracking grades?** Notion —
  the filtering and single-source-of-truth benefits compound fast.
- **Not sure?** Try the hybrid: capture on paper during the day, and spend five
  minutes each evening moving deadlines into a Notion tracker that gives you
  the week-at-a-glance view.
