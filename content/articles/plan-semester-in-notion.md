Most stress in a semester isn't caused by the work itself — it's caused by
*surprise* work. Three deadlines you forgot about, all landing in the same
week. Planning the whole semester up front in Notion is a two-hour investment
that removes almost all of those surprises.

## Step 1: Dump every date from every syllabus

Before the second week of classes, open each syllabus and enter **every**
graded item into one Assignments database — quizzes, essays, exams,
presentations, participation checkpoints. Yes, all of them. This is tedious
once and quietly life-changing afterward.

Include the `Due` date and the `Weight` (% of grade) for each. If a date is
"TBD," put your best guess and mark it to confirm later.

## Step 2: Look at the semester on a calendar

Switch your Assignments database to a **Calendar** view. Now zoom out and look
at the whole term. You're looking for **collision weeks** — where multiple
heavy items (high `Weight`) land within a few days of each other.

Every semester has two or three of these. The students who handle them well
aren't smarter; they just *saw them coming*.

## Step 3: Add "start" dates, not just due dates

A due date tells you when something is late. A **start date** tells you when to
begin so it isn't. For anything worth more than ~10% of your grade, add a
second date property called `Start` set a week or two before the deadline.
Filter a view to show what you should be *starting* this week, not just what's
due.

## Step 4: Protect the collision weeks

For each collision week you found in Step 2, do the front-loadable work early.
Reading, outlining, research, and study guides can almost always be pulled
forward. You can't move the exam, but you can move the preparation.

## Step 5: Do a 10-minute weekly review

Once a week — Sunday evening works well — open your planner and:

1. Check what's due in the next 7–10 days.
2. Confirm any "TBD" dates that got announced.
3. Move anything you didn't finish and adjust start dates.

Ten minutes a week keeps the whole system honest. Skip it for three weeks and
the plan quietly rots.
