## Why Standard Planners Fail the ADHD Brain
For many people with ADHD, the problem isn't a lack of tools; it's that most productivity systems are designed for neurotypical brains. They assume you have consistent executive function, stable motivation, and the ability to prioritize linearly. When you try to force an ADHD brain into a rigid, multi-step Kanban board or a complex spreadsheet, you often end up with more anxiety and less progress.

A functional ADHD focus system needs to be low-friction, visual, and forgiving. It should reduce the cognitive load of deciding *what* to do next. Here is how to build that system using Notion, focusing on simplicity over complexity.

## Step 1: Create Your 'Now' Dashboard
The biggest mistake is trying to manage your entire life in one view. Instead, create a single page called 'Today' or 'Focus'. This page should only contain three elements: 

1. **The One Thing:** A single text block where you write the *one* most important task for the day. This reduces decision fatigue.
2. **Time-Blocking Slots:** Instead of a to-do list, create three 90-minute blocks. Label them 'Deep Work,' 'Admin/Shallow Work,' and 'Rest/Walk.' 
3. **A 'Brain Dump' Section:** A simple bulleted list at the bottom where you can quickly type out any distracting thoughts without organizing them. This keeps your working memory clear.

## Step 2: Implement the 'Do or Drop' Rule
ADHD brains often suffer from task accumulation. If a task doesn't get done, it stays on the list, growing larger and more intimidating. In your Notion system, implement a strict rule: if a task hasn't been started within 48 hours, it is either deleted or moved to a 'Someday' archive.

To make this easy, use Notion’s button functionality. Create a button labeled 'Done' and another labeled 'Drop'. When you click 'Drop', the task is automatically moved to an archive database. This creates a sense of closure and prevents the 'to-do' list from becoming a source of shame.

## Step 3: Use Gamification and Visual Cues
ADHD brains respond well to immediate feedback and visual stimulation. Use Notion’s toggle lists to hide complex subtasks. When you complete a main task, check the box and watch it strike through. This provides a small dopamine hit.

You can also use color coding not for categories (which is often arbitrary), but for energy levels. Mark tasks that require high focus in red, and low-focus tasks in green. This helps you match your task selection to your current energy state, rather than fighting against it.

## Keeping It Sustainable
The goal is not to build a perfect system, but a usable one. Review your dashboard every Sunday for five minutes only. If a feature isn't being used, remove it. The best ADHD system is the one you actually open.
