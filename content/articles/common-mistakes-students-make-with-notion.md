Many students download Notion with high hopes, only to abandon it two weeks later. Why? Usually, the tool itself isn’t the problem; the way it’s implemented is. Here are the five most common mistakes that turn a helpful organizational tool into a source of stress, and how to fix them.

## Mistake 1: Over-Customizing the Interface
Notion is infinitely customizable, which is both its strength and its weakness. A common error is spending hours changing fonts, colors, and icons before adding any actual content. If you are tweaking your dashboard more than you are using it, you are procrastinating. Fix this by using the default font and color scheme for the first month. If you still love the tool after a month, then customize. Prioritize function over aesthetics initially. A plain, working system is better than a beautiful, empty one.

## Mistake 2: Creating Too Many Databases
Students often create a separate database for assignments, another for notes, another for projects, and another for exams. This fragments your data. If you need to look up a note related to an assignment, you have to check two different places. Instead, use a hierarchical structure. One main "Courses" database should contain sub-databases for "Notes" and "Assignments" within each course page. This keeps related information together and ensures that when you search for a term, all related data appears in one context.

## Mistake 3: Ignoring Mobile Access
Notion is a web-based application, but many students only use it on their laptops. The problem is that students are often on the go, in lecture halls, or in libraries where laptops are not convenient. If you cannot easily access your notes on your phone, you won’t use them. Fix this by downloading the Notion mobile app and testing it. Ensure your databases load quickly on mobile. If a database is too complex, it may lag on mobile. Simplify your views so they are readable on a smaller screen. Your system must work as well on your phone as it does on your desktop.

## Mistake 4: Not Using Templates Wisely
Many students try to build everything from scratch because they think it’s more impressive. This is inefficient. Notion has a library of free templates, and many creators share them. However, the mistake is adopting a template that is too complex for your needs. If a template has 20 pages and 50 databases, it is likely too much. Look for simple, modular templates. Or, build your own simple template for recurring tasks, like "Weekly Review," and reuse it. Don’t reinvent the wheel for every single week.

## Mistake 5: Treating Notion as a Storage Unit, Not a Workflow
Notion is not just a digital filing cabinet. It is a workflow tool. If you are just dumping PDFs and screenshots into pages without processing them, you are using it wrong. The value comes from the actions you take within the tool. For example, instead of just saving a lecture recording, create a page for that lecture and write three key takeaways. Instead of just saving an article, add a note about how it relates to your current paper. If you are not actively engaging with the content inside Notion, you are just creating a larger, more complex mess. Your goal should be to use Notion to process information, not just store it.
